## The Dual System: Western Comma vs. Korean Speech

Standing in front of a coffee shop menu or checking apartment listings in Seoul often leads to a sudden cognitive wall.

You see a price like **100,000원** on a screen, your eyes process three zeros after a comma, and your brain instantly translates it as "one hundred thousand."

Then you try to say the price aloud in Korean, and your mental engine stalls completely.

This is the **Dual System** problem: Korean banks, menus, and price tags **display** numbers using Western 3-digit comma formatting (**100,000**), but Korean **speech** reads numbers using a 4-digit framework (**십만**).

Your eyes are seeing 3-digit visual groupings, but your vocal cords need 4-digit spatial math.

This visual-verbal mismatch is why reading big numbers feels like an impossible puzzle to Western learners.

![Western 3-digit comma vs Korean 4-digit Man block on a 100,000원 price tag](/images/posts/korean-numbers-dual-system-comma-vs-man.png)
*Western cognition: comma every 3 digits. Korean speech: **10 / 0000** → **십만** — two systems on one price tag.*

## The 4-Zero Mindset (The "Man" Unit Box)

To break free from the Dual System, you need to upgrade your mental grid from 3-digit chunks (Thousand, Million) to 4-digit building blocks.

In Western languages, new number words unlock every 3 zeros (10³ = Thousand, 10⁶ = Million, 10⁹ = Billion).

In East Asian spatial logic, the primary counting engine unlocks a new major unit every **4 zeros**:

* **만** (**10,000** = 10⁴): Ten Thousand
* **억** (**100,000,000** = 10⁸): Hundred Million
* **조** (**10,000,000,000,000** = 10¹²): Trillion

Instead of forcing **10,000** into "ten thousands," Korean speech treats **만** as a single primary container.

Think of **만** as an independent base unit, just like "thousand" functions in English.

## The "4-Digit Slash" Method (Concept & Lightweight Demo)

How do you bridge the gap when looking at printed prices?

The fix is simple: ignore the printed 3-digit commas and draw an invisible **slash every 4 digits from right to left**.

Let's walk through a quick example with the number **53,451**:

![The 4-Digit Slash Method applied to 53,451 — 5/3451 to 오만 삼천사백오십일](/images/posts/korean-numbers-4-digit-slash-53451.png)
*Count 4 digits from the right → **5 / 3451** → **오만** + **삼천사백오십일***

1. **Ignore the printed comma** and count 4 digits from the right end, then place your slash: **5 / 3451**.
2. **Left side ('5')**: Attach the 4-digit unit **만**. This gives you **오만** (50,000).
3. **Right side ('3451')**: Read the remaining 4-digit block normally as **삼천사백오십일** (3,451).
4. **Combine them**: **오만 삼천사백오십일** (*O-man sam-cheon-sa-baek-o-sip-il*).

By slicing the number into 4-digit containers, you bypass the visual distraction of Western commas instantly.
