## 12 Years of English, but Frozen in Front of a Native Speaker

I spent 12 years in South Korea’s traditional education system during the 1980s and 90s. I memorized vocabulary lists, mastered sentence-parsing mechanics, and eventually scored a 910 on the TOEIC exam.

On paper, I was fluent. In reality? I couldn't speak a single spontaneous sentence to a native speaker.

Every time I tried to talk, an internal **"Grammar Censor"** took over. I was constantly calculating sentence structures and checking verb tenses in my head before opening my mouth. I knew all the rules, yet I was completely mute.

If you are learning Korean right now through heavy grammar books, you know this exact frustration.

---

## "You Know What I'm Sayin'?" — The Sound Over Rules Realization

What saved my relationship with language wasn't a textbook—it was Hollywood movies.

I remember hearing characters throw around phrases like *"You know what I'm sayin'?"* (or simply *"You na'msayin'?"*). I didn't translate the words or parse the auxiliary verbs. Instead, my brain instantly linked the raw sound to the emotional context: *Ah, in this exact situation, people make this sound.*

That was my first realization as a creator: **Language is not an equation to solve; it is living sound tied to visual context.**

---

## The Curator's Hypothesis: Learning vs. Acquisition

This observation led me to the core philosophy behind Uncle Hangul: the difference between **Language Learning** and **Natural Acquisition**.

> **Natural Acquisition simply means acquiring language like a child.**
>
> Children don't study grammar tables or memorize flashcards with translation keys. They observe visual cues, absorb spoken rhythm, and connect sound directly to meaning through play.

To test this, I didn't just read theories. For the past six years, I had the privilege of observing a real-world experiment right at home: **watching my own children learn to speak from scratch.**

Watching them naturally absorb complex speech patterns purely through context and Comprehensible Input (CI) turned my hypothesis into absolute conviction.

---

## Not a Guru, But an Experimenter

> **I am not an academic language professor, nor do I want to be a guru standing at a podium.**
>
> I view myself as a **Curator and Experimenter**. My goal is to design visual, auditory, and digital systems that bypass your internal grammar censor and help you absorb Korean with zero friction.

And to do that, one medium isn't enough.
