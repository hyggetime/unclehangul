**Standard Korean dictionaries often translate both *An* (**안**) and *Mot* (**못**) as "not"**, but swapping them in conversation can accidentally make you sound surprisingly cold or rude. Here is the simple mental model: *An* (**안**) expresses your personal choice or will ("I don't" / "I choose not to"), while *Mot* (**못**) expresses a physical limit, lack of ability, or unavoidable circumstance ("I can't" / "I am unable to").

---

## Situation 1: An (안) – Personal Lifestyle or Habitual Choice

**Use *An* when expressing a personal preference**, routine, or intentional choice that is not caused by an allergy, physical limitation, or outside obstacle.

### Dialogue Example (Expressing a personal habit)

* **Korean:** **저 아침 안 먹어요.**
* **Pronunciation:** [Jeo a-chim an meok-eo-yo.]
* **Literal Translation:** I breakfast not eat.
* **Natural English:** "I don't eat breakfast."
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **저** (I) | **아침** (Breakfast) | **안** (Not / Don't — choice) | **먹어요** (Eat)

---

## Situation 2: Mot (못) – Physical Limits or Lack of Ability

**Use *Mot* when you actually want to do something**, but your physical body, low tolerance, or lack of skill prevents you from doing it.

### Dialogue Example (Explaining spice tolerance)

* **Korean:** **저 매운 거 못 먹어요.**
* **Pronunciation:** [Jeo mae-un geo mot meok-eo-yo.]
* **Literal Translation:** I spicy thing cannot eat.
* **Natural English:** "I can't eat spicy food."
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **저** (I) | **매운 거** (Spicy thing/food) | **못** (Cannot — ability) | **먹어요** (Eat)

---

## Situation 3: Mot (못) for Polite Refusal – Social Nuance

**Understanding the gap between these two words is vital** when declining plans with friends or coworkers. If a friend invites you out and you reply with **안 가요**, it literally communicates "I am choosing not to go with you," which comes across as blunt and dismissive. Saying **못 가요** communicates "I wish I could go, but my schedule or circumstance stops me," making your refusal polite and considerate.

### Dialogue Example (Politely declining an invitation)

* **Korean:** **죄송해요, 오늘 약속에 못 가요.**
* **Pronunciation:** [Joe-song-hae-yo, o-neul yak-sok-e mot ga-yo.]
* **Literal Translation:** Sorry, today appointment to cannot go.
* **Natural English:** "I'm sorry, I can't make it to the plans today."
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **죄송해요** (I'm sorry) | **오늘** (Today) | **약속에** (To plans/appointment) | **못** (Cannot — circumstance) | **가요** (Go)

---

## Uncle Hangul's Quick Recap

* **안** (An — will & choice): Use for personal habits, choices, and routine preferences ("I choose not to").
* **못** (Mot — capability & circumstance): Use for lack of skill, physical limitations, or unavoidable scheduling conflicts ("I am unable to").
* **Social Pro Tip:** When declining an invitation or turning down a request in Korea, always default to **못** to show that your heart wants to say yes, but your circumstance says no.
