**Standard Korean textbooks will tell you to say *Sil-rye-ham-ni-da* when you want to say "Excuse me."** But if you step onto a subway in Seoul, you will almost never hear locals use **실례합니다**; instead, everyone relies on *Jam-si-man-yo* (**잠시만요**) or *Jam-kkan-man-yo* (**잠깐만요**). While both literally translate to "just a short moment," they double as "Excuse me" and "Wait a second" depending entirely on the situation.

---

## Situation 1: Jam-si-man-yo (잠시만요) – Passing Through a Crowded Subway or Elevator

**When you need to push past people on a crowded train, elevator, or bus**, *Jam-si-man-yo* (**잠시만요**) is your ultimate public survival phrase. You do not need extra fluff or long explanations—saying it clearly and politely lets people know you need space to pass.

### Dialogue Example (Getting off a crowded train)

* **Korean:** **잠시만요! 지나갈게요.**
* **Pronunciation:** [Jam-si-man-yo! Ji-na-gal-ge-yo.]
* **Literal Translation:** A short moment please! I will pass through.
* **Natural English:** "Excuse me, coming through!"
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **잠시만요** (Just a moment / Excuse me) | **지나갈게요** (Passing through)

---

## Situation 2: Jam-si-man-yo (잠시만요) – Buying Time at the Register

**Korean is a high-context language**, meaning people omit obvious details whenever the situation speaks for itself. If you are rummaging through your bag for a credit card at a cafe checkout, you do not need to explain "I am looking for my card." You simply say **아, 잠시만요!** because the cashier already sees what you are doing.

### Dialogue Example (Buying time at checkout)

* **Korean:** **아, 잠시만요!**
* **Pronunciation:** [Ah, jam-si-man-yo!]
* **Literal Translation:** Ah, a moment please!
* **Natural English:** "Ah, give me just a second!"
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **아** (Ah) | **잠시만요** (Just a second / Hold on)

---

## Situation 3: Jam-kkan-man-yo (잠깐만요) – Stopping Someone or Pausing an Action

*Jam-kkan-man-yo* (**잠깐만요**) shares the exact same core dictionary meaning ("a short moment"), but it feels slightly more direct and conversational. It is ideal when you need to pause an action, hold up a conversation, or stop someone who is walking away.

### Dialogue Example (Stopping someone who left something behind)

* **Korean:** **어, 잠깐만요! 이거 두고 가셨어요.**
* **Pronunciation:** [Eo, jam-kkan-man-yo! I-geo du-go ga-syeoss-eo-yo.]
* **Literal Translation:** Oh, a short sec please! This left behind and went.
* **Natural English:** "Wait a second! You left this behind."
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** **어** (Oh) | **잠깐만요** (Wait a sec) | **이거** (This) | **두고 가셨어요** (Left behind)

---

## Uncle Hangul's Quick Recap

* **Same Core Meaning:** Both *Jam-si* and *Jam-kkan* mean "a short moment." At their core, both phrases mean "Just a second."
* **잠시만요** (Jam-si-man-yo): Slightly smoother and softer. Best used as "Excuse me" when asking for space in public or politely buying time.
* **잠깐만요** (Jam-kkan-man-yo): Slightly more casual and spoken. Perfect for catching someone's attention or saying "Wait a sec!"
* **High-Context Rule:** When the situation is clear to everyone, omit extra words. A simple **잠시만요!** is all you need to get your point across.
